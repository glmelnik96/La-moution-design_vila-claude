# Motion art-direction — "GRID & RUPTURE"

A design-philosophy manifesto for live AE motion work, authored with the
`anthropic-skills:canvas-design` method (write the philosophy first, then express
it with master-level craft). This is the house aesthetic for showcase/motion
pieces in this skill. Read it before art-directing any animation. It blends the
two directions the user chose: **A — Swiss / editorial** and **C — Bold / kinetic**.

---

## The movement

**GRID & RUPTURE.** Rigid Swiss order, violently interrupted. The frame is a
strict editorial grid — real columns, real margins, everything hung from baselines
and left edges, nothing lazily centered. Then a single kinetic force ruptures that
calm: one word slams in oversize, one accent color cuts across the neutral field,
one element overshoots its mark. Meaning lives in the *tension* between the
discipline of the grid and the violence of the rupture. Structure communicates;
the accent punctuates. Never the reverse.

## Space & composition

A true grid governs everything — a 12-column feel with generous outer margins
(≥120px on a 1920 frame) and a consistent baseline. Compose with **asymmetry and
negative space**, not centering: hero type anchored to the left margin, small
reference markers pinned to corners, vast quiet zones that let one gesture breathe.
Every element aligns to the grid or deliberately breaks it — never floats
arbitrarily. Corner markers, thin rules, scene indices ("01 — 06"), and clinical
coordinate labels frame the work like a page from an imaginary technical journal.
The composition must read as the product of countless hours of refinement, every
alignment the result of painstaking attention.

## Type

Two voices, extreme contrast. **Hero:** one or two words, enormous, bold, tight
tracking, set on the grid — sculptural typography that carries the whole frame.
**Labels:** tiny, wide-tracked, uppercase, mono-ish, whisper-quiet — figure
numbers, scene names, technical notes. Nothing in between. No decorative middle
weights. Type *is* the composition, not a caption on top of it. Scale contrast
(hero ~10–20× the label) and weight contrast do the work. Meticulously set, never
default-and-centered.

## Color & material

Ruthless restraint: **ink**, **paper**, **one signal accent**, and greys between.
Ink near-black `#0E0E10`; paper warm off-white `#F2EEE4`; the signal a single hot
vermillion `#FF3B21`. Optionally one cold electric blue `#2E5BFF` used *once* for a
kinetic counterpoint — never a rainbow. The accent is a scalpel: a slashing block,
a single filled dot, one ruptured word. Depth comes from craft, not clutter —
subtle gradient in the field, a soft glow behind the accent, a fine film grain
overlay, and real **motion blur** on everything that moves. Flat, grain-less,
blur-less motion is forbidden.

## Rhythm & motion

Motion honours the same order-vs-rupture law. Reveals use the premium ease
`0.16,1,0.3,1` (start-key low influence, land-key ~90) so things *arrive* with
weight, never linear. Kinetic hero type slams in fast then settles with a small
overshoot (spring). Supporting elements enter in a tight **stagger** (2–3 frame
offsets) so the eye reads sequence, not chaos. Between scenes, a hard accent-color
block **wipes** across the frame for continuity — the rupture becomes the
transition. Always motion blur; never ship a physical move that is stiff or flat.

## Craft standard

Every frame must survive an art director's eye, not just "it rendered." After each
capture, judge: is the composition intentional (grid, asymmetry, negative space) or
lazily centered? Palette restrained to ink+paper+one accent, or stock-rainbow? Type
crafted (weight, tracking, scale contrast) or default Arial? Is there depth
(gradient, glow, grain, motion blur)? If it looks generic, it *is* generic — fix it
before presenting. The finished piece must look like it was labored over by someone
at the absolute top of the field: master-level execution, painstaking attention,
undeniably designed.
